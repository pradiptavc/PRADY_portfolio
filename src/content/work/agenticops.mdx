## The context

Security and network operators work inside a stream of alerts, configuration changes and competing priorities. AI can analyse more than a person ever could, but analysis alone does not earn the right to act.

<aside class="pull-quote">The experience had to make intelligence feel like leverage—not another source of uncertainty.</aside>

## The challenge

Early concepts focused on what the agent could do. Customer conversations exposed a different question: how would an operator know when to trust it? We needed to make the agent’s scope, evidence, progress and proposed action continuously legible.

## My role

I led the experience from problem framing through prototype, executive alignment and delivery. I connected customer research, product telemetry and technical constraints into a design strategy, then worked across product, data science and engineering to define the human–agent relationship.

## The moves that mattered

### 01 — Give every agent a bounded job

We framed agent activity as an explicit job with a goal, scope, status and owner. This replaced the ambiguity of an open-ended assistant with an operational unit people could understand and manage.

### 02 — Show the chain of reasoning through evidence

Rather than presenting a confident answer alone, the experience exposed the signals, affected policies and decision trail behind it. Operators could inspect before they accepted.

### 03 — Design interruption and recovery

Trust depends on the ability to pause, redirect or stop. Those controls were treated as core product behavior, not edge cases.

### 04 — Connect insight to accountable action

The design preserved a clear handoff between what the system found, what it proposed and what a human authorised. Automation accelerated the work without erasing ownership.

## Outcome

The policy-analysis capability reached 5,016 customer tenants and analysed approximately 128,000 unique security policies. More than 8,500 analyses were manually initiated—an important signal that customers were not merely exposed to the feature; they chose to use it.

## Leadership reflection

AI trust is interaction design at system scale. It comes from visible boundaries, inspectable evidence, meaningful control and honest communication about uncertainty.

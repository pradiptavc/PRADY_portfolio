## The context

In large organisations, a firewall policy is rarely owned by one person. Authors, reviewers, administrators and approvers may work across different teams and time zones. Their tools, however, often treat each edit as an isolated action.

<aside class="pull-quote">This was not a ticketing problem. It was a coordination problem with security consequences.</aside>

## The tension

Parallel changes could collide. Responsibility became unclear as work moved between people, and urgent edits bypassed the context needed for a safe review. The interface showed configuration state, but not the social state surrounding it.

## My role

I led the workflow model and interaction design, mapping the real paths changes took across teams. I aligned product and engineering partners around ownership as a platform concern and used prototypes to expose policy decisions that could not be resolved in requirements documents alone.

## The moves that mattered

### 01 — Make work visible as a shared object

We represented a change as a durable object with purpose, affected policies, participants and status. Everyone could refer to the same source of truth.

### 02 — Separate authorship from authority

The workflow distinguished who proposed a change, who could evaluate it and who could deploy it. This supported real organisational controls without making every user learn the permission model.

### 03 — Surface collisions before deployment

Potential overlap and dependency were brought into the review moment, when teams could still coordinate safely.

### 04 — Preserve the decision trail

Comments, evidence and approvals remained attached to the work, creating continuity when responsibility moved between people.

## Outcome

The redesign turned fragmented edits into an understandable change lifecycle. It gave teams a clearer basis for collaboration while supporting the governance expected in high-stakes environments.

## Leadership reflection

Good enterprise collaboration is not a layer of comments placed on top of work. It is a product model that makes ownership, consequence and transition explicit.
